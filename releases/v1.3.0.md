## 🔎 Why the old phrases had repeated words

The pre-1.3.0 generator used a **136-word custom list**, drew 11 words **with replacement**, then
appended a checksum word. That gives:

- **≈78 bits of entropy** (11 × log₂136), below the 128-bit target;
- a **34% chance of at least one repeated word** (observed: "eclipse" three times);
- no structural reason for repeats to be rare.

The wordlist itself had no duplicates — this was a sampling-design weakness, now fixed.

## 🔐 What changed

- **BIP-39 English** generation: 2048-word standard, 128-bit CSPRNG entropy + 4-bit checksum,
  **12 distinct words** (repeats rejected/regenerated).
- Legacy 136-word phrases and all existing vaults/backups remain compatible; recovery validation
  accepts either scheme, and PBKDF2 derivation is unchanged.
- **Recovery-kit rotation** in Settings: generates a fresh BIP-39 phrase and salt, re-wraps the same
  vault key, and leaves records, fingerprint and master password unchanged. The old phrase retires
  for this vault; old backups remain restorable with the phrase under which they were exported.
- Full recovery/encryption audit, including AES-256-GCM record/AAD, VMK wrapping, PBKDF2 600k,
  recovery-at-rest wrapping, backup envelope, and biometric keystore review:
  [`docs/AUDIT-2026-11-15-recovery-and-encryption.md`](https://github.com/cipherwell/Vault-Zero/blob/main/docs/AUDIT-2026-11-15-recovery-and-encryption.md).
